# How to actually write one of these

The process, written down, because the engine kept skipping the only step that
matters. Two failures got us here:

- **"Six O'Clock Means War"** — unreadable. Fixed by demanding clarity.
- **"First names on the board"** — perfectly clear, and *nothing funny happens
  in it*. Tralalero wants a treadmill. He ends up signed up for squats. He
  stands still. The end.

The second one is the more important failure, because it proves clarity was
never the actual problem. **The engine was never asked for a JOKE.** It was
asked for a want, an obstacle and a choice — and a want, an obstacle and a
choice with no comic engine is a police report.

---

## The step that was missing: find the joke BEFORE writing

You do not start with a situation. You start with a character's flaw, and then
you go looking for the one situation that punishes them for it.

### 1. Pick one character and name the flaw in five words

Not their description — the thing they *cannot help doing*.

- Tralalero Tralala: **has to tell you his 5k time**
- Orangina: **cannot refuse credit**
- Tracy Beaker: **cannot be seen being kind**
- Jeremy Clarkson: **refuses to admit effort exists**
- Bombardiro Crocodilo: **cannot use a lighter weight**

### 2. Build the trap

Ask: *what is the worst possible situation for someone with exactly that
flaw?* That question is the whole job. It is where the comedy comes from, and
almost nothing else is.

- The bragger → a situation where the brag is *overheard by the wrong person*
- The credit-taker → credit for something that turns out to be a disaster
- The one who can't be seen being kind → getting caught being kind

### 3. Make the character spring their own trap

This is the difference between funny and merely unfortunate. The flaw must be
the *cause*, not the context. Bad luck is not comedy — self-infliction is.

> Tralalero brags about his sub-nineteen 5k. Erling Haaland hears it, is
> impressed, and signs him up as a squat partner. **He talked himself into leg
> day.** He did it to himself with his own mouth.

### 4. Find the reversal

The strongest comic shapes, in rough order of reliability:

1. **They get exactly what they wanted, and it's a punishment.**
2. **Their lie comes true in the worst way.**
3. **They win, and the prize is worthless.**
4. **They avoid the thing, and avoiding it costs more than doing it.**

### 5. Name the joke in one sentence, before writing a word

> *The funny is: he brags his way into the exact session he was avoiding.*

If you cannot write that sentence, you do not have an episode yet. Go back to
step 2. **This is the check that would have stopped both failures.**

### 6. Escalate in threes

Setup → the same thing again, worse → the break. Three beats, and the third one
turns. Two is not a pattern; four is a list.

### 7. Write the last line first

The punchline is the destination. If you know where you're landing, everything
before it is just getting there — and you'll cut the parts that don't.

### 8. *Then* write it plainly

Short sentences, ordinary words, one thing at a time, nothing referred to that
hasn't been shown. But plain is the *delivery*, not the substance. A flat
retelling of nothing is not an improvement on a confusing retelling of nothing.

---

## The two tests, at the end

**Point at the laugh.** Quote the funniest line. If you can't find one, it
isn't finished — that is a rewrite, not a matter of taste.

**Say what it cost.** Somebody must lose something, ideally their dignity, and
ideally in front of other people. "He did not move" is not a cost.

## Worked, on the failure

Take the exact same setup and apply the process:

| Step | "First names on the board" (what shipped) | With the process |
|---|---|---|
| The flaw | not identified | has to tell you his 5k time |
| The trap | he wants a treadmill | the brag is overheard by the one person who'll act on it |
| Self-inflicted? | no — Ballerina has the pen | yes — his own mouth does it |
| The reversal | none | he gets admiration, and admiration costs him leg day |
| The joke, named | never named | *he brags his way into the session he was dodging* |
| The cost | nothing, he stands still | his own record is now the reason he can't run |
| Last line | "He did not move." | he has to say the time out loud again, to get out of it, and it works — he's now down for Thursday as well |

Same characters. Same gym. One is a story about a man near a treadmill; the
other is about a man destroyed by his own favourite fact.
