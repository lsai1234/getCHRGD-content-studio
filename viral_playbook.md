# The Viral Playbook — shapes that have already banged

This is the evidence library for the concept stage. Every pattern in here is a
post *shape* that has repeatedly performed on TikTok in and around the
gym/fitness space — not a theory, a precedent. When the engine proposes a
concept it must anchor it to one of these shapes (or to a live trend the seed
carries) and say why the shape fits.

**This is a living file.** When you see a carousel bang — yours or anyone's —
add its shape here with a line on why it worked. When a pattern stops
performing, cut it. The engine is only as sharp as this library.

How to read each entry: **the shape** (how the carousel actually runs, slide
by slide), **why it bangs** (the psychological engine), and **the CHRGD fit**
(how it plays in this brand's world).

---

## Identity & recognition shapes

**1. The archetype taxonomy ("types of guys at the gym")**
Shape: slide 1 names the cast ("the 5 guys at every gym at 6am"); one archetype
per slide, each drawn with ONE hyper-specific tell (not a description — a
behaviour); last slide asks which one you are / which one to tag.
Why it bangs: self-categorisation is the strongest comment engine there is —
everyone is one of them and knows which, and tagging a mate as one is a
ready-made social move. The specificity of the tells is the whole game.
CHRGD fit: gym floor, supplement aisle, pre-workout rituals, locker room.

**2. POV: the lived micro-moment**
Shape: slide 1 drops you inside a scene in second person ("POV: you said one
rep left 4 sets ago"); slides escalate the scene beat by beat; payoff is the
feeling named exactly.
Why it bangs: self-referential immersion — the viewer isn't shown content,
they're placed in a memory. Recognition-shock ("too true") drives shares.
CHRGD fit: the 6am alarm, the pre-workout kick-in, the last-set tunnel vision.

**3. The unsaid thing, said ("we all do it, nobody admits it")**
Shape: slide 1 confesses a universal-but-private behaviour ("the warm-up set
you do so the guy waiting knows you're nearly done"); slides pile on sibling
behaviours, each more precise; CTA is a confession invite.
Why it bangs: naming something felt-but-never-said produces the deepest
recognition there is. Comments fill with "I feel seen" + confessions.
CHRGD fit: gym self-consciousness, caffeine dependency rituals, mirror checks.

**4. Red flags / green flags**
Shape: instant-judgment framework applied to a niche context ("gym red flags"
/ "green flags in a training partner"); one flag per slide, escalating from
agreeable to contentious; last flag is deliberately arguable.
Why it bangs: everyone applies the frame to people they know in real time;
the arguable final flag starts the comment fight on cue.
CHRGD fit: training partners, gym etiquette, supplement habits.

## Debate & indignation shapes

**5. The slightly-wrong ranking (tier list)**
Shape: slide 1 declares a definitive ranking of something the audience has
strong feelings about; slides reveal tiers with one indefensible placement
placed confidently; CTA: "what did I get wrong?"
Why it bangs: being wrong on the internet outpulls being right — people are
COMPELLED to correct a confident mistake. The wrongness must be arguable, not
stupid.
CHRGD fit: exercises, protein flavours, gym chains, pre-workout rituals,
cardio machines.

**6. The myth the industry sells you**
Shape: slide 1 states the belief everyone holds as if it's fact, then cracks
it ("'toning' isn't a thing — here's what's actually happening"); middle
slides show the mechanism with real specifics; payoff reframes what to
actually do; strictly claim-safe.
Why it bangs: indignation at having been misled + insider status for knowing
better. Shares as "I told you so" ammunition.
CHRGD fit: supplement myths (educational/observational only), training myths,
diet-culture leftovers.

**7. The hill I'll die on (hot take played straight)**
Shape: slide 1 is one contrarian sentence delivered with total confidence
("machines beat free weights for most people"); slides make the honest case
with specifics; final slide doubles down and invites the fight.
Why it bangs: a confident minority position forces a pick-a-side response;
both agreement and outrage are engagement.
CHRGD fit: training dogma, gym culture sacred cows, supplement snobbery.

## Story & payoff shapes

**8. Expectation vs reality (the split)**
Shape: alternating slides or split frames — what you thought it'd be like vs
what it's actually like ("first month at the gym: the plan / the reality");
comedy lives in the gap; payoff is warm, not mocking.
Why it bangs: amusement + self-recognition without cruelty; extremely
shareable to the person you lived it with.
CHRGD fit: new year gym-goers, first pre-workout experience, bulk season.

**9. Things I wish I knew (scars, not lectures)**
Shape: slide 1 stakes the credential in one line ("5 years lifting — what I'd
tell myself at day 1"); each slide is one lesson phrased as a mistake actually
made, with the specific cost; payoff slide is the one lesson that matters.
Why it bangs: advice packaged as scar tissue is saved, not skimmed — high
saveability, aspirational identity share ("us at day 1").
CHRGD fit: training, recovery, caffeine timing, wasting money on the wrong
supplements (observational).

**10. The 'wait, WHAT?' mechanism (awe fact done visually)**
Shape: slide 1 is the fact stated at its most jarring ("your body burns more
calories asleep than on the treadmill — sometimes"); middle slides unpack the
mechanism visually, one beat per slide; payoff is what it means for you.
Why it bangs: prediction error + awe; shares as social currency ("did you
know…"). Must be TRUE and checkable — one shaky fact kills trust.
CHRGD fit: the body/energy/caffeine/recovery science the evergreen lane digs
up.

**11. The receipt breakdown (what you're actually paying for)**
Shape: slide 1 shows the price everyone pays ("your £4.50 energy drink");
slides itemise what's actually in it / what it actually costs, with exact
numbers; payoff reframes value.
Why it bangs: exact numbers are believed, and money makes it personal;
comment fight between defenders and the betrayed.
CHRGD fit: energy drinks vs pre-workout per-serving maths, gym memberships,
supplement markups — careful: informative, never a competitor attack.

## Culture & time shapes

**12. The hours club (time-based identity)**
Shape: claims a specific time as an identity ("the 5:30am gym crowd is a
different breed"); slides draw the rituals of that hour with insider detail;
CTA: which club are you in.
Why it bangs: time-of-day is an identity people are proud of; insider details
prove membership. This shape is the account's own biggest win (the 2am
England-game post).
CHRGD fit: early sessions, late sessions, the lunchtime rush, match nights.

**13. Overheard at the gym (found dialogue)**
Shape: real-sounding fragments of gym conversation, one per slide, no
commentary — the curation IS the joke; payoff is the most unhinged one.
Why it bangs: found-object comedy feels discovered rather than written;
screenshots travel to group chats whole.
CHRGD fit: bro-science quotes, PT clichés, supplement aisle wisdom.

**14. The absurd commitment bit**
Shape: takes one niche behaviour and plays it dead straight to an absurd
conclusion ("a mortgage application for the squat rack, since you're living
there"); the format never winks; visual gag carries it.
Why it bangs: committed absurdity is the most screenshottable comedy shape on
the platform; amusement + "the audacity" shares.
CHRGD fit: rack hoggers, gym-bag over-packers, shaker-bottle collections.

## Using this library (rules for the engine)

- Every proposed concept names its shape from this file — or names the live
  trend it rides instead. "Original" concepts with no precedent need a
  reason to exist; default to proven shapes executed brilliantly.
- The shape is a chassis, not a script: the specificity of the tells, the
  exactness of the numbers, and the named-unnamed lines are what separate a
  banger from a template fill-in.
- Never propose two concepts on the same shape in one fan-out — the point of
  competing takes is competing engines.
- Match shape to goal: comment-fights → 4/5/7; shares → 1/2/3/13/14;
  saves → 9/10/11; all of them still need a hook that stops the thumb.
